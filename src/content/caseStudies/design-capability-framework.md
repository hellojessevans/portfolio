## Background

At the beginning of 2024, Nine's product design teams lacked a shared capability framework, aligned levelling structure, or consistent approach to growth and performance conversations.

This became particularly visible following employee engagement feedback, where one of the lowest-scoring areas related to clarity around expectations, progression, and development.

Together with the Head of Product Design for Streaming, I led the creation of a completely bespoke capability and levelling framework designed specifically for our teams and ways of working.

## Approach

Rather than adopting an off-the-shelf framework, we built the model from first principles.

This involved extensive research, synthesis, and collaborative working sessions to:

- Define capability areas
- Align expectations across levels
- Articulate behavioural and craft standards
- Create clearer progression pathways
- Support more structured development conversations

The work was completed before AI tools became integrated into our day-to-day practice, meaning the framework was entirely researched, written, and shaped manually around the realities of our organisation and teams.

The framework also became the foundation for:

- Formalised goal setting
- Quarterly growth check-ins
- Performance reflections
- More structured feedback conversations across the design practice

## Outcome

The framework introduced significantly more clarity and consistency around expectations, progression, and development across the design organisation.

![Product Design Expectations Matrix](/case-studies/design%20capability/Matrix%20Full.png)

It created a shared language for capability and growth, helped managers and designers have more meaningful development conversations, and established a stronger foundation for scaling design quality and practice maturity over time.
