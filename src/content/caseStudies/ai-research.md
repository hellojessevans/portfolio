## Background

I needed to quickly validate whether a new quiz experience was genuinely improving the user experience compared to the existing format before we rolled it out more broadly across Nine's publishing products.

The decision carried additional weight because the experience was intended for some of Nine's highest-engagement editorial products, where usability expectations and brand fit are particularly important. While the team had strong opinions internally, we lacked enough validated customer insight to confidently move forward.

Normally, a research cycle like this would have taken significantly longer to coordinate, run, synthesise, and package into stakeholder-ready outputs. But we didn't have the luxury of time - we were working against committed launch timelines with an external vendor already engaged, meaning we effectively needed a confident decision within 24 hours or the new experience would move ahead regardless.

## Problem

The challenge wasn't just speed - it was maintaining research quality while dramatically reducing turnaround time.

I needed:

- Fast participant recruitment
- Usable insights within hours, not weeks
- Enough signal to support confident product decisions
- Outputs stakeholders could quickly understand and act on

At the same time, I wanted to explore whether AI could meaningfully reduce operational overhead within the research process without compromising the quality of thinking, moderation, synthesis, or decision making.

## Approach

To support the work, I used an internally developed AI agent called Ravi - a system I've been helping shape as part of Nine's broader AI design capability work.

Rather than automating research end-to-end, the process was intentionally collaborative. Ravi accelerated setup, synthesis, and packaging, while I remained responsible for research strategy, participant quality, question design, interpretation, and decision framing.

![AI-assisted testing plan and research questions](/case-studies/ai%20research/AI%20research%20-%20approach%201.png)

Working across a tightly compressed timeframe, I:

- Translated research objectives into structured testing plans
- Generated and refined participant screeners
- Crafted moderated and unmoderated test questions
- Rapidly synthesised participant responses
- Identified patterns, friction points, and behavioural signals
- Packaged findings into a stakeholder-ready narrative

![Participant screener design](/case-studies/ai%20research/AI%20research%20-%20approach%202.png)

The biggest shift wasn't replacing research thinking - it was removing operational drag so more time could be spent on higher-value analysis, interpretation, and decision quality.

## Outcome

I was able to move from research setup to stakeholder-ready findings in under 24 hours, feeding clear recommendations directly back into the product and design teams ahead of launch decisions.

![Usability testing results and key findings](/case-studies/ai%20research/AI%20research%20-%20outcomes.png)

Because synthesis and packaging time were significantly reduced, I was also able to test with more participants than would normally be feasible within the same timeframe, increasing confidence in the quality of the signal while still delivering polished, decision-ready outputs.

More broadly, the project became an important internal example of how AI can augment, rather than replace, design and research practice. The work was later selected as part of Nine's AI showcase at our bi-annual digital conference, where I presented the approach as an example of how AI was being applied practically across product and design workflows.
