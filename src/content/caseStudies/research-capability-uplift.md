## Background

When I joined Nine's Publishing design team, all usability testing and customer research sat within a centralised Audience Insights function. Product teams would submit briefs and learning objectives, then wait for research to be planned, moderated, synthesised, and returned as a final report.

While the model worked well for larger initiatives, it created a dependency that slowed smaller product decisions and limited direct exposure between designers and customers.

At the same time, the design team had a strong appetite to build their own research capability - not to replace the insights team, but to create faster feedback loops, become more confident speaking to users directly, and embed research more naturally into day-to-day product delivery.

## Approach

My focus was on building confidence and capability through hands-on coaching and embedded practice, rather than introducing a rigid process from the top down.

I worked closely with designers across the full research lifecycle:

- Selecting and implementing tooling (Askable, Optimal Workshop, etc.)
- Writing screeners and recruitment criteria
- Crafting open, non-leading interview questions
- Structuring usability tasks and discussion guides
- Moderating interviews
- Synthesising insights
- Shaping playback and stakeholder reporting

In the early stages, I pace-set heavily, co-writing, reviewing, and running the first few sessions alongside designers so they could learn by doing. Over time, ownership naturally shifted into the teams themselves.

## Outcome

Research is now a regular and embedded part of the product process across the team, spanning both moderated and unmoderated usability testing.

Over the last few years, designers across the team have independently engaged with more than 1,000 users through ongoing research activities, averaging roughly 40 participant interactions per month outside of larger quantitative studies.

More importantly, research shifted from a centralised service into a shared product capability, giving teams faster access to insight and much closer connection to customer needs.

One of the clearest signs the shift had worked was when Product Managers started proactively approaching the design team asking if we could run testing for upcoming ideas and initiatives. Research had moved from being something external and occasional to something embedded within day-to-day product thinking and decision making.
